01 About

Trained in
biology.
Fluent in data.

I began in the wet lab — learning how organisms work at the molecular level, how enzymes fold, how cells signal, how systems fail. That training didn't end when I picked up Python. It changed how I ask questions of data.

Most data scientists work upward from the numbers. I work inward from the biology. The model is only as good as the scientist who knows what the features actually represent, what the noise comes from, and what a biologically implausible result looks like.

My current focus is applying machine learning to life-science datasets — climate-health intersections, biological signal classification, and research automation. I publish work publicly and build every project for reproducibility.

04 Thinking
Essay — Biology × Computation

Why the wet lab
made me a better
data scientist

Most ML practitioners approach biological data as just another tabular dataset. What they miss is that every feature is a measurement with a protocol, a margin of error, and a biological mechanism behind it.

Perspective — On Models

The question isn't
accuracy — it's
what are you measuring?

A 95% accurate model on a poorly framed problem is worse than a 70% model that asks the right question. Metric selection is a scientific claim, not a technical choice.

Reflection — Process

Building in public
when there's nothing
impressive yet

The consumption trap is real — courses, tutorials, reading without shipping. The only way out is to publish something flawed and let the discomfort of public imperfection accelerate the learning.
